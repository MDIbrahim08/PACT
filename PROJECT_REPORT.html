Executive Summary

In India, over 63 million Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) contribute approximately thirty percent of the national Gross Domestic Product and nearly half of total exports. Despite their economic significance, more than ninety percent of domestic business-to-business transactions are conducted informally through casual WhatsApp messages, telephone conversations, and regional voice recordings. Small enterprise proprietors consistently avoid traditional multi-page legal agreements because drafting contracts requires formal legal counsel, entails prohibitive expenses ranging from three thousand to fifteen thousand rupees per deal, and carries social stigma where requesting a written contract is perceived as an expression of personal distrust.

As a direct consequence of operating on informal handshakes, small suppliers face severe vulnerabilities. When corporate or wholesale buyers delay or default on payments, suppliers lack legally admissible documentation to enforce claims before judicial magistrates or statutory arbitration councils. According to official industry assessments, Indian MSMEs suffer from an estimated 10.7 Lakh Crore Rupees in delayed and locked working capital.

Project PACT resolves this crisis by introducing an invisible, automated commercial notary. PACT operates directly within the merchant's natural communication workflow. It ingests conversational agreements, audits them against the essential requirements of Section 10 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, detects ambiguous terms that would void an agreement under Section 29, applies statutory credit ceilings under the MSMED Act, 2006, and generates tamper-evident electronic evidence certificates compliant with Section 63(4) of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023. By packaging these checks into polite, automated summaries dispatched through standard WhatsApp, PACT eliminates social friction while delivering full legal enforceability.

1. Selected Problem Statement

Official Problem Definition: Transforming informal commercial communication (including messaging platform text, voice recordings, and trade discussions) into legally binding, court-admissible commercial contracts for Indian Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises under the Indian Contract Act, 1872 and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.

The project addresses three fundamental legal and commercial breakdowns in informal Indian trade:

  • The Contractual Formality Barrier: The conventional process of drafting formal legal agreements is socially awkward, procedurally slow, and commercially impractical for high-velocity daily wholesale trade.
  • The Electronic Evidence Inadmissibility Trap: Ordinary screenshots of chat conversations are routinely rejected as unauthenticated hearsay by courts and arbitration tribunals because digital images can be forged or manipulated effortlessly without leaving visible artifacts.
  • The Asymmetry of Bargaining Power: Small vendors lack the leverage to demand prompt payment terms from larger institutional buyers, leading to severe payment delays that exceed statutory limits established by Parliament.

2. Problem Understanding and Context

Understanding the day-to-day operations of Indian wholesale trading clusters reveals why existing legal technologies have failed to penetrate the MSME sector. In industrial clusters such as Surat (textiles), Moradabad (brassware), Rajkot (engineering components), and Delhi (packaging), commerce is relationship-driven. When a buyer places an order over WhatsApp with a short voice note, requesting a signed ten-page legal agreement causes immediate friction, often prompting the buyer to redirect the order to a competitor.

Consequently, merchants rely on mutual trust. However, when economic shocks or commercial disputes occur, this trust breaks down:

  • Uncertain Terms under Section 29: Informally negotiated terms frequently contain ambiguous statements such as "we will settle accounts after delivery" or "payment will follow after festival sales." Under Section 29 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, agreements whose meaning is not certain or capable of being made certain are void ab initio.
  • Judicial Requirements under Section 63(4) of Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023: The Indian judicial system enforces strict criteria for electronic evidence. For an electronic record to be admitted into evidence without producing the original computer system, the party must present a certificate identifying the electronic record, describing the device used, confirming lawful control, and establishing system integrity. Raw printouts or screenshots without cryptographic hashing fail this legal test.
  • Statutory Protections Ignored: The Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006 provides strong legal rights, including a mandatory forty-five-day payment ceiling under Section 15 and compound interest at three times the Reserve Bank of India bank rate under Section 16. However, suppliers rarely invoke these provisions because they lack dated, verified records of the agreed terms.

3. Proposed Solution

Project PACT introduces an automated, neutral commercial assistant and electronic notary that integrates seamlessly into informal trade. The system converts raw conversations into legally verified agreements through five sequential steps:

  1. Informal Ingestion: The system accepts voice notes in regional languages or plain text conversation transcripts directly from messaging tools.
  2. Semantic Term Structuring: The system extracts the vital commercial terms: participating parties, item description, exact quantities, pricing, delivery schedule, and credit terms.
  3. Deterministic Statutory Audit: The extracted terms are audited against statutory rules to confirm compliance with Section 10 and Section 29 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872 and Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006.
  4. Cryptographic Evidence Sealing: The validated contract terms are converted into a canonical representation and sealed with a dual-node SHA-256 cryptographic digest, generating an electronic certificate compliant with Section 63(4) of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.
  5. Automated WhatsApp Dispatch: The system generates an actionable link that opens a direct WhatsApp conversation with the buyer, delivering a concise, polite contract summary bearing the cryptographic verification seal.

The "Blame-the-Bot" Social Paradigm: To eliminate social friction, PACT enables the supplier to present the verification as an automated accounting procedure: "Our automated accounting system requires confirmation of the delivery date to generate our GST e-stamp certificate. Please confirm so the order can be processed." This preserves the personal relationship while securing legal enforceability.

5. System Workflow and Functional Overview

The system workflow is designed for maximum simplicity for the merchant, with rigorous legal checks occurring automatically behind the scenes.

Step-by-Step Functional Workflow

  1. Input Capture: The merchant speaks a brief voice note in their native tongue or pastes recent chat messages into the PACT interface.
  2. Transcription and Structuring: The input is converted into clean text and structured into standardized commercial fields: Buyer Identity, Seller Identity, Scope of Goods, Total Consideration, Delivery Milestone, and Settlement Terms.
  3. Statutory Five-Pillar Audit: The system verifies the five statutory requirements under Section 10 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872:
    • Offer and Acceptance: Mutual agreement on goods and specifications.
    • Lawful Consideration: Monetary value or commercial payment.
    • Competency of Parties: Natural or registered commercial entities.
    • Free Consent: Absence of coercive or unfair settlement clauses.
    • Lawful Object: Goods and transactions permitted under Indian trade law.
  4. Ambiguity Detection and Credit Auditing: If critical milestones are vague, the system highlights missing terms. If payment terms exceed forty-five days, the system applies Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006, capping the period and calculating statutory interest.
  5. Cryptographic Hashing and Non-Repudiation: A normalized data string is processed through the SHA-256 algorithm to generate a unique digital fingerprint. If any character in the contract terms is altered subsequently, the resulting hash changes completely, providing mathematical proof of tampering.
  6. Electronic Evidence Certificate Generation: An electronic certificate is compiled containing device identifiers, time stamps, hash signatures, and legal declarations in strict accordance with the Fourth Schedule of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.
  7. Automated WhatsApp Dispatch: A pre-formatted message is generated containing the summary terms and cryptographic seal, ready for one-touch delivery to the recipient's phone number.
  8. Independent Verification Portal: Any judge, arbitrator, or party can paste the document text and hash into the public verification portal to immediately confirm document authenticity.

6. Operational and Technical Feasibility

The architectural strategy prioritizes high availability, low operational cost, and zero software friction for trade counterparties:

  • Zero-Install Counterparty Access: The single greatest barrier to legal technology adoption is requiring both trading partners to create accounts. PACT delivers the final contract directly through standard WhatsApp using standard universal telephony links, meaning buyers require zero onboarding.
  • Progressive Web Architecture for Godown Operations: Wholesale markets and warehouse godowns often suffer from erratic cellular connectivity. The PACT interface is engineered as a Progressive Web Application with offline service caching and local cryptographic calculation capabilities.
  • Privacy-by-Design and Zero Data Retention: To protect sensitive commercial trade secrets, PACT does not store merchant invoices, prices, or customer identities on central database servers. Transactions are processed in volatile execution memory and sealed with client-side cryptography. When an enterprise resets or uninstalls the application, all local cache keys are permanently purged.

10. Utilization of Artificial Intelligence and External Services

A critical architectural decision in Project PACT is the strict separation between non-deterministic language processing and deterministic legal computation:

  • Natural Language Understanding and Speech Processing: Modern artificial intelligence models are deployed strictly to handle perceptual tasks: transcribing spoken regional dialects, interpreting conversational vernacular phrasing, and identifying entity names from chat histories.
  • Deterministic Statutory Computation: No artificial intelligence model is permitted to evaluate contract validity, compute interest rates, or determine legal admissibility. All legal evaluations, statutory threshold comparisons, date validations, and cryptographic hash operations are executed by deterministic programmatic rules. This completely eliminates legal hallucinations, ensuring that every certificate generated is legally accurate and reproducible.

Implementing automated commercial notarization delivers measurable socio-economic benefits:

  • Unlocking Delayed Working Capital: By providing small suppliers with verifiable proof of delivery dates and agreed payment schedules, PACT dramatically strengthens the supplier's position when filing claims with the Micro and Small Enterprise Facilitation Councils (MSEFC / MSME Samadhaan).
  • Democratizing Legal Protection: Legal security has traditionally been an expensive privilege reserved for large corporations with dedicated legal departments.   • Accelerating Dispute Resolution: When commercial disputes are accompanied by cryptographically verified certificates and audit trails, judicial magistrates and arbitration mediators can bypass months of evidentiary hearings regarding whether an agreement existed, shortening dispute resolution from years to weeks.

13. Challenges Faced and Solutions Implemented

During the design and construction of the platform, the engineering team overcame four major challenges:

  • Challenge 1: Regional Vernacular Ambiguity. Informal voice notes frequently combine multiple languages and casual colloquialisms that confuse standard legal parsing.
    Solution: Engineered a dual-stage pipeline that first normalizes regional audio into standardized commercial entities before passing them to the statutory validator.
  • Challenge 2: Counterparty Reluctance to Adopt New Software. Wholesale buyers consistently refuse to download proprietary applications or complete complex registration forms.
    Solution: Adopted a zero-barrier architecture that transmits certified notices directly through standard WhatsApp without requiring the recipient to install any software.
  • Challenge 3: Compliance with New Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam Standards. Ensuring that electronic certificates strictly adhere to the updated requirements of Section 63(4) of the 2023 statute rather than obsolete provisions.
    Solution: Formatted all electronic evidence certificates in exact conformity with the Fourth Schedule conditions, including device identification, custody certification, and cryptographic hash verification.
  • Challenge 4: Vulnerability to Cryptographic Timing Attacks. Standard string comparison algorithms are vulnerable to timing analysis attacks when validating cryptographic hashes.
    Solution: Implemented constant-time comparison algorithms using native cryptographic timing-safe methods to ensure mathematical non-repudiation.
